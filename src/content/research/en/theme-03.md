With the rapid advancement and widespread adoption of data science, developing effective strategies for collaboration with increasingly sophisticated AI systems and generating experimental datasets of sufficient scale for machine learning have become essential challenges even for experimental chemists. We aim to promote the effective integration of informatics into the development of conjugated polymer materials and electrochemical processes, guided by the following two principles.
1. Efficient exploration of unexplored chemical spaces through iterative machine learning
Even when the predictive accuracy of a machine learning model is limited, it can still serve as a valuable tool for prioritizing experiments and improving the efficiency of exploring unknown chemical spaces. We seek to accelerate materials and process development through an iterative approach in which new experimental data are systematically acquired from unexplored regions, including negative results arising from inaccurate model predictions, to progressively improve model performance and predictive reliability.
2. Physics- and chemistry-informed informatics guided by domain knowledge
A central challenge in materials informatics is identifying novel materials with properties that surpass those of known materials. However, this task is often hindered by the curse of dimensionality and the inherent difficulty of predicting exceptional properties or anomalous behavior. To address these limitations, we aim to establish informatics strategies that leverage researchers' theoretical understanding of physics and chemistry to define scientifically meaningful search spaces. By focusing data acquisition and analysis within these physically and chemically constrained domains, we seek to achieve more efficient and reliable materials discovery.
